What creative decisions do the tasks actually test?

The set covers several real professional skills, but task count and family labels overstate its variety. Repeated image-and-record layouts, identity recovery and short video assembly account for much of the work. Distinctive tasks should be selected by the decisions below, with separate checks for input readiness.

Main findings

The current MOTION scope is narrower than its titles. All 48 requested MP4 outputs have duration bounds within 6-15 seconds: 31 music-only and 17 silent. The briefs exclude speech. A podcast title or narration label therefore does not establish podcast or narration-editing coverage.

Repeated formats are not automatically redundant. Technical masking in PHOTO-14 differs from technical layout in LAYOUT-27; physical adaptation in VECTOR-12 differs from reproducing a crest. The question is whether a task adds a new judgment that changes the result, rather than another set of records.

The pilot concentrates on photographic consistency and campaign layouts. PHOTO-06 and PHOTO-26 add especially different work. PHOTO-20 is the first slot to reconsider for breadth; PHOTO-08, PHOTO-10 and PHOTO-24 need a deliberate reason to all occupy a small pilot.

Review method

For each task: main decision, tradeoff, nearest comparisons, recommendation and an exact supporting passage. Read the brief before the title; distinguish routine repetition from a different judgment.

New pilot candidates below are provisional. Asset usability, feasibility, rights, typography access and verifier completeness were not re-audited in this coverage review.

Coverage gaps and biases

  • Speech and dialogue editing. The 48 requested MP4 outputs have only music or silent audio modes. Current briefs exclude speech. Podcast conversation selection, narration editing, dialogue repair and speech/music mixing are not represented by those titles.
  • Longer temporal structure. All requested MP4 duration bounds sit between 6 and 15 seconds. Full lesson pacing, sustained demonstrations, longer documentary structure and interview development are not established by this set.
  • Original concept development. Explicit examples exist: LAYOUT-12 asks for three different directions, VECTOR-04 asks for two new illustrations, VECTOR-09 permits concept geometry refinement, and MOTION-11 asks for an authored reveal. Much of the remaining work has predetermined copy, identity and source treatment. This is a qualitative observation, not a measured count of all concept-capable tasks.
  • Typography and imagery carry much of the variation. Different products, people and palettes often sit inside the same photograph-plus-record workflow. Meaningful craft differences such as hair edges, metal reflections or glaze retention should be named and scored directly.
  • Interactive or functional design. A website/page composition in these briefs is normally a static visual export. It does not demonstrate a working website, interaction design or responsive implementation.
  • Uneven emphasis on legacy-file recovery. Lost logos, old printer PDFs and record reconciliation recur across families. These are valid production challenges but should not dominate every category or be mistaken for a new creative capability each time.
